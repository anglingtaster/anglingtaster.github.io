Appellate decisions

Matters before the Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court and Appeals Court in which Mr. Anglin served as counsel.

418 Mass. 220 (1994) · Supreme Judicial Court

Dalis v. Buyer Advertising, Inc.

As prevailing counsel, supported by an amicus filing from the American Civil Liberties Union, Mr. Anglin secured the right to a jury trial for plaintiffs in Massachusetts sex-discrimination cases. The decision prompted a redefinition of practice before the Massachusetts Commission Against Discrimination and a deeper understanding of the breadth of the jury trial right in Massachusetts.

Read the opinion
Reaffirmed ten years later Given the reach of the decision, the Supreme Judicial Court on its own initiative invited briefing on whether Dalis was correct. In Stonehill College v. MCAD, 441 Mass. 549 (2004), four consolidated cases, a reconstituted Court concluded that Dalis was correctly decided.

A life beyond the courtroom

Gerald Anglin grew up in a suburb of Boston. He lost his father at twelve and left high school a few years later; eventually he, his two sisters and his brother all graduated from Northeastern University, then a working-class university. He commuted and worked throughout college, and while a student designed and taught a civil-liberties course for motivated high-school students at MIT.

At Case Western Reserve University School of Law he was President of the Student Body and a published, two-year Associate Editor of the Law Review.

Family and pursuits

He has been married for 42 years and has five adult children and six grandchildren, and stays close to dozens of relatives across generations in the Boston area. For many years he coached young athletes.

Today he pursues golf, photography, reading and travel, having visited some fifty cities across Western Europe, and has spent more than thirty summers on the outer Cape.
